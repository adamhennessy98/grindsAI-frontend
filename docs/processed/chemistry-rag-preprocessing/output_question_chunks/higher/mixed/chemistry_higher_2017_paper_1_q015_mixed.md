# Question

11.  Answer any two of the parts (a), (b) and (c).                                          (2 × 25)



      (a)  Some of the blue pigment azurite used by an artist in a picture painted in 1600 has
          changed over the years into green malachite according to the following balanced
           equation.
                  2Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2 (s)  +  H2O (l) → 3Cu2CO3(OH)2 (s)  +  CO2 (g)

                                    azurite (blue)                              malachite (green)

            Analysis shows that the only copper-containing pigments in the painting are azurite
         and malachite and that the picture now contains 3.81 g of copper.

                 (i)   What mass of pure azurite contains 3.81 g copper?                               (9)
                  (ii)   Calculate the maximum mass of malachite that will eventually be formed
              from this mass of azurite.                                                          (9)
                  (iii)  What is the volume, measured at s.t.p., of carbon dioxide that would
              be released from the painting in the formation of this mass of malachite?      (6)
               (iv)  Suggest a way that further deterioration of the painting by this chemical
                 reaction could be prevented or delayed.                                          (1)





      (b)   Define electronegativity.                                                                 (6)
         Draw dot and cross diagrams to show the bonding in a molecule of
                 (i)    nitrogen trifluoride (NF3),
                  (ii)   boron trifluoride (BF3),
                  (iii)   chlorine monofluoride (ClF).


         Use electron pair repulsion theory to predict the shape of a molecule of
          boron trifluoride.                                                                  (12)

         Use electronegativity values to
               (iv)   indicate on your bonding diagram for chlorine monofluoride any full or partial
                charges that you would expect to result from the formation of the bond between
                 chlorine and fluorine,
             (v)   predict the type of bonding in chlorine monofluoride.                           (7)
