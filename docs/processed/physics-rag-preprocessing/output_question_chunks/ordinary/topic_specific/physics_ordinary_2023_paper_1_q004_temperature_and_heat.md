# Question

3.   A student carried out an experiment to measure the specific latent heat of fusion of ice.
    He added ice at 0 °C to water in a copper calorimeter.

     The following results were recorded.

       Mass of empty copper calorimeter                        = 0.083 kg

       Mass of calorimeter + water                             = 0.181 kg

       Mass of ice                                           = 0.008 kg

           Initial temperature of calorimeter + water                  = 23 °C

         Final temperature of calorimeter + water + melted ice        = 16 °C

     Below is a diagram of the apparatus used in this experiment.



        A
                                                                                 cloth with
                                                                       crushed ice



                                              calorimeter



         B
                                          water with
                                            crushed ice



        (i)   Name the piece of equipment labelled A.

         (ii)  Name the piece of equipment labelled B.                                                (6)

         (iii)  Why did the student crush the ice before adding it to the calorimeter?

       (iv)  What was the cloth used for in this experiment?                                        (8)

       (v)   Calculate the fall in temperature of the calorimeter and water.

       (vi)   Calculate the mass of the water.                                                         (8)

        (vii)  Using the formula mcΔθ and your answers to (v) and (vi), calculate E, the heat lost by
          the water and the calorimeter.                                                           (9)

        (viii)  Calculate the rise in temperature of the ice.

       (ix)   Calculate the value for l, the specific latent heat of fusion of ice.
          Note: E = micel + micecwaterΔθice                                                            (9)

      specific heat capacity of copper = 390 J kg–1K–1; specific heat capacity of water = 4180 J kg–1 K–1
