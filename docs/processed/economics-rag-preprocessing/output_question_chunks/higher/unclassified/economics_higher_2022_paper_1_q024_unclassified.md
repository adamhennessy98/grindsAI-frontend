(a)  Rent increases in so-called rent pressure zones (RPZs) are to be capped in line with
      inflation, and a ban on evictions has been put in place for the rest of the year (2021)
     under new laws set to be introduced by the Government.
                                                   Adapted from The Irish Times

(i)  What are the likely impacts of this government intervention on social sustainability?      1 x 8
                                                                              1 x 4
        •  Reduced homelessness. Restrictions on prices and elimination of the risk of
             eviction should help to curb the risk of homelessness for a significant number of
               Irish people.
        •  Improved standard of living. If citizens are required to spend less of their
          income on paying high rents, then they can afford to spend more on other
           goods and services thereby improving their standard of living.
        •  Reduced inequality. Through introducing these measures, the government will
          hope to narrow the gap between the rich and the poor and make life more
             liveable for the most vulnerable members of society.
        •  Increased ability to save for a mortgage. If there is reduced pressure on rents it
         may afford renters more of an opportunity to save additional income which can
          be put towards a down payment on a house.
        •  Greater security for the economically vulnerable. Those citizens who have
            experienced significant pressure from an economic perspective during the global
          pandemic will welcome these measures as it may allow them more breathing
           space and an opportunity to better plan for the future.

(ii)  Other than rent caps, suggest two other government interventions which could help      1 x 8
     reduce inequality in accessing the Irish housing market.                             1 x 4


        •  Improved access to social housing. If the government can help to provide more
              social housing it will help to protect more of Ireland’s most economically
            vulnerable people. It may also help to relieve some of the pressure on the wider
            housing market.
        •  Improvements in the Help to Buy scheme e.g. increase above €30,000 or allow
            to be used on second-hand properties. If the requirements and rewards within
            the HTB scheme are amended to better reflect the needs of first-time buyers in
               Irish housing market, it may help to reduce inequality.
        •  Reduction in mortgage lending requirement for low-income earners. If the
            ECB/Central bank reduces the required deposit or adjusts the stringency of
            other requirements it may increase the number of people who gain access to
            the housing market.
        •  Increases in the minimum wage. If the minimum wage is increased, it will give
            greater scope to low-income earners to save additional earnings which can be
           put towards a deposit for a house.
        •  Tax breaks for low-income earners other than those offered in the Help to Buy
           scheme.
        •  Empty home tax. If an empty home tax is introduced, it will act as a disincentive
             for landlords to leave their properties empty for long periods of time. This may
           encourage some of these homeowners to sell these properties which would
            increase the supply of houses in the market.
        •   Derelict building incentives. If the government provides incentives for
            upgrading derelict buildings across the country, it will help to recover a lot of
             buildings which were lost in the ‘Great Recession’.

(ii)   Suggest strategies that could be employed by the Irish government and Irish citizens to
     aid with the transition to a low carbon economy.

     Government:                                                              1 x 7
                                                                                            (4+3)        •  Increased incentives to purchase electric cars. If the government can provide
             incentives, financial or otherwise, to Irish citizens to make the switch to electric
             vehicles it may increase the uptake of these vehicles in Ireland.
        •  Increased environmental audits of production methods of Irish firms. If the
           department of the environment is more stringent in its review of how firms do
            business in Ireland it may deter firms from producing in environmentally
           harmful ways.
        •  Increased penalties for firms which have a negative environmental impact.
            Firms which produce in environmentally harmful ways could be hit with large
              financial penalties by the government.
        •  Tax breaks for firms which produce in environmentally friendly production
           methods. This financial incentive may be enough to encourage a firm to amend
             their production practices.
        •  Increased funding for research and development in green areas. The
           government could help support university initiatives across Ireland to try and
            help plan for a green future.
        •  Increased funding for educational programmes for the youth. Increased
            funding for educational programs for the youth may help to prepare the next
            generation on how to approach this crisis.
        •  Improve infrastructure for electric vehicles. If the government provides more
            charging points for electric vehicles it will better allow Irish motorists to
             transition to less environmentally harmful vehicles.


      Citizens:                                                                  1 x 7
                                                                                            (4+3)        •  More ethically aware consumption of goods. If consumers are more particular
           about the products they purchase and are more aware of the production
            processes involved in producing these products, they can become an active part
             of reducing carbon consumption in Ireland.
        •  Concerted efforts to reduce consumption of goods which are harmful to the
           environment. Some consumers are happy to consume products regardless of
             their environmental impact. If this pattern can be changed it will speed up this
             process. E.g. swapping to electric vehicles.
        •  Increase efforts to recycle products. Efforts to make the life of a product longer
          and keep it out of landfill help to reduce carbon emissions into the environment.
           Recent trends such as upcycling have been helpful on this front.
