     (i)    The principal budget factor is that aspect of the business that limits or constrains
          the acƟvity of the ﬁrm and prevents the conƟnuous growth of the business.
          Sales volume is the most common principal budget factor and is usually the
          starƟng point in the budgeƟng process.
         Other examples include:

         The output capacity of the machinery – if the machinery is limited in its output
            this can limit the amount of producƟon that can take place in the business.

         The amount of raw materials available- if a ﬁrm has diﬃculty sourcing raw
          materials this can aﬀect the ﬁrm’s ability to produce goods.

         The availability of skilled labour. This can aﬀect the ﬁrm’s ability to produce
          goods.



     (ii)     Uncontrollable costs are costs over which the manager of a cost centre has no
          control and therefore, cannot be held responsible for variances in these costs e.g.
           rates to the local authority are uncontrollable.
